## What it is now
Settings > Gameplay > Physics > Physics mode = Test bench (real actuators) turns on a real actuator model for any mech
whose legs list actuators with `bench` figures in `data/parts.json` (the Juggernaut Model 2 and the stand-in). Game mode is
untouched (all game-mode suites still pass).

- **data/bench.json** (new registry; every constant lives here): motor model, thermal, failure, supply (battery bus), gait,
  grounds, readout thresholds, and `entries` = the designs the bench can load (one per mech id).
- **scripts/mech/bench_rig.gd** (BenchRig, pure logic, no nodes): per joint a torque-speed line (stall = rated x 1.6, zero
  torque at the no-load speed, which follows bus voltage), electrical power = mechanical + copper heat (heat_w x
  (torque/rated)^2, so holding still costs heat) + idle, winding temperature with cooling, thermal foldback from 120 C to
  half torque at 155 C where it trips (resets 15 C under the derate point), shock failure (overload time weighted by how far
  over 2.2 x peak; latched until reset), battery with internal resistance (sag, brownout share when asked for more than 400 kW,
  empty), jump energy. Figures come from parts.json: rotary hip yaw/pitch/ankle (`rated_torque_nm`, `speed_rad_s`, `power_w`,
  `heat_w`) and the knee as a linear ram (`rated_force_n` x `moment_arm_m`); the older linear templates still resolve.
- **scripts/mech/bench_driver.gd** (a child node "Bench" the stand-in mech adds in `_ready`): each tick it takes every leg's
  real ground reaction (spring force, plus the leg's share of the drive push) and works out the torque on hip yaw, hip pitch,
  knee and ankle from the pose, estimates joint speeds from the pose, and steps the rig. Back to the mech: `leg_force_cap`
  (a leg cannot push harder than its weakest pitch joint can hold; a compressing leg is back-driven so speed does not limit
  it), `drive_force_cap` and `drive_power_w` (the push is bounded by torque left after holding the body up and by bus power),
  `speed_factor` and `min_swing_s` (steps take as long as the hip joints need), `bill_jump` (a charged jump is paid from the
  battery), `shock_demand` (a leg closed past 60 % of its rest length hits its end stop: failure force = stopping its share
  of the body over 6 cm, so a hard landing breaks joints, walking never does). F6 resets the bench (battery, heat,
  failures), F7 shows or hides the readouts.
- **scripts/ui/bench_hud.gd**: panel top right: battery %, volts, amps, kW, brownout flag; a grid of 4 legs x 4 joints, each
  cell a torque bar (tick = what the load asked), a temperature bar (tick = derate point), "kNm kW C" text and a status
  (derated, saturated, tripped, failed, no power); list of joints out and the last three events. Created by the driver for
  the player's mech (not in headless runs).
- **Settings** (Gameplay > Physics > "Test bench" group): design (options from bench.json entries), failures, cooling x,
  battery size kWh, charge at reset %, bench readouts. Input actions `bench_reset` F6 and `bench_readouts` F7 (System).
- **standin_mech.gd hooks** (all behind `bench_on()`): spring force cap and demand record per leg, drive caps, step swing
  time, speed cap x speed_factor, jump bill, telemetry "bench".
- **parts.json**: new templates `act_linear_heavy_hip` (500 kN, lever 0.9 m) and `act_linear_heavy_knee_big` (700 kN) for the
  stand-in's hips and knees: the old 150 kN templates could not hold the stand-in's 5.2 t on its 3.8 m legs, so in bench mode
  it collapsed at spawn and `test_physics:test_bench_mode_is_slow` failed. `tests/test_registry.gd` expects 700000 for its knee.

## Reasoned, not verified
- The HUD has never been looked at: no screenshot or bot run yet (it is not created in headless runs). Layout is a guess.
- All bench.json numbers are drafts (cooling share, derate floor 0.5, shock factor 2.2, end stop 6 cm, battery 60 kWh 96 V
  400 kW, peak factor 1.6). The 96 V bus is what parts.json's power_w / current_a imply (hundreds of amps per joint).
- Joint speeds are finite differences of the IK pose, smoothed over 0.12 s; swing legs can exceed the motors' speed (the
  swing time floor slows steps but landings and re-seats are still kinematic). A swing joint shows "saturated" then.
- Standing and walking never get hot at 5.4 t (hottest joint 20.8 C after a 15 s walk) because the walk is so slow and the
  actuators were sized for 12.25 t. Overheating needs a heavier load, a lower Cooling setting or a long run. Decision for
  Emerson/B1: re-size the actuators in parts.json to the realistic mass, or leave them as the generous drafts.
- Yaw torque and drive-force readouts use the drive's push split evenly over the loaded legs.
