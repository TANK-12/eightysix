## 1. His first 30 seconds

Exo Lab opens full screen (fullscreen: auto). The home page has two big cards, Real-world tester and Game, then six model cards with no pictures. Four of the six are greyed out as "planned". First reaction: "where's the spider mech? show me the suit". He clicks Open the tester. A few seconds later the VRoid girl appears in a black carbon suit with cyan lines, a helmet that looks like a swim cap, and two mint-green rods sticking up out of her shoulders (shots2/a1-full-front.png). "Ok it actually fits her, sick... what are the green pipes?" Then he reads the readout: "Gimbal-lock margin 0.59", "Exo joints 12° · -4° · 9°" (shots/2-tester-rom.png), and asks "what the hell is gimbal lock margin". He opens the editor, drags a power slider, and grins as the verdict, cards and game stats change with green arrows. A few minutes in, he picks the lite suit: the girl is in her blouse and underwear with dark legs. Then he presses Game and plays the stock suit, which jumps lower than he can.

## 2. What he would love

- The full suit really hugs the body. The shells were built from the VRM's own surface, nothing passes through the skin, and the cyan accent lines look good (/tmp/exolab-emerson-m-zoom.png, right side).
- The knobs he likes: sliders with number fields and × multipliers, double-click to reset, Undo/Redo, Save as, and a live re-run. The editor has six tabs plus a materials chart (shots/4-editor-materials.png).
- The "Compared with baseline" and "Game stats from these parts" tables with % arrows. With power ×2, sprint goes up 22% and two-arm lift goes up 106% (/tmp/exolab-emerson-m-left.png). This is the bench-to-game link he asked for.
- The verdicts are in plain words, e.g. "of the 363 arm directions a person can reach, the exo follows 98%". The range map tries a pose when you click a cell (shots2/b3-lite-rom-map.png). Pose mode has an "In the hand" kg slider, and motions play back with charts.
- The results are honest and worth arguing over. The full suit fails the overarm throw on elbow speed. The lite fails the 10 kg carry and the 12 kg lift.
- Each material, drive, mechanism and test is one JSON file, which follows his registry rule.

## 3. Missing, confusing, ugly, or not what he asked for

- **The lite suit.** He asked for "separate parts so skin stays visible". Instead the skirt is hidden, the VRoid underwear shows under a grey hip belt, and the arms and legs are tinted dark (/tmp/exolab-emerson-m-zoom.png left, shots2/g1-lite-front.png). The cause: tintUndersuit() in static/js/suit.js and dressUnder() in static/game/character.js tint the skin whenever the sidecar notes contain the word "undersuit". static/assets/suits/exo_lite.json has the same note text as exo_full.json, so the lite gets tinted too. After his earlier run-in over anime content, a girl shown in her underwear in his exo app will make him wince.
- **The game doesn't play what he built.** The Game nav link and the home card open plain game.html (static/index.html, lines 25 and 46). So the game always plays the baseline exo_full. Its start menu still says "Every number in this game comes from the suit you built in the tester", while the stats panel shows "Profile baseline" (shots2/h1-game-start.png).
- **The exo soldier is weaker than a person without a suit.** Derived sprint is 5.84 m/s and jump is 0.38 m. The unsuited human in data/game/base.json does 7.0 m/s and 0.45 m. Normal lift is 12.5 kg, and Strength mode only reaches 25 kg. In the tester, the full suit gives a warning holding a 10 kg carry (elbow at 164%) and lifting a 12 kg box. The maths is honest but the fantasy is wrong: in AW and Crysis the suit makes you more than human.
- **Power is free in the editor.** I set power ×20 on every powered drive. The suit still weighed 20.7 kg and the box lift passed. In the game, sprint hit its 14 m/s cap, jump went to 1.99 m and lift to 151 kg. Nothing warned (POST /api/run and /api/derive). A "real-world tester" that hands out a 20× motor with no added weight is the kind of fakery he usually catches.
- **The mechanism looks like mint-green toy pipes.** tint() in view3d.js colours every passing part COL.pass (0x59d499). So on a healthy suit, green rods stick up like antennae against the carbon and cyan (shots2/a1, a2-full-back.png).
- **The shoulder is hard to see.** The Shoulder camera sits about 1.4 m away, so her face fills the middle of the view (shots2/a4-full-shoulder-raised.png). The 3D panel is only about 480 px tall in a 1500x950 window. Turning Suit off leaves her bald with charcoal skin (shots2/a5-full-shoulder-nosuit.png).
- **Planned models show stale content.** Picking one keeps the previous girl, pose box and frame numbers on screen under "Spider mech: planned" (shots2/c1-spider-planned.png).
- **Mixed signals and jargon.** The headline says "Pass" next to an orange "Closest to gimbal lock 0.10" card. Jargon is everywhere: J1/J2/J3, "J2 side -1", "Pod radius", "First axis · Plane -30°", "backdrivable quasi-direct drive" (/tmp/exolab-emerson-m-editor.png).
- **The editor is still a form, not "a great editor".** Nothing in the 3D view can be clicked (view3d.js has no raycaster), and you set the mechanism's shape by typing numbers.
- **"Especially the shoulder".** Four shoulder mechanisms exist, but comparing them means saving four profiles and comparing them two at a time.
- **Four of six models are only cards** (spider, biped, cyber arm, cyber leg). The plan puts them in chunks 3 and 4, but he will ask anyway.
- **The game (the helper's first pass).** The range is grey and flat and the soldier is small on screen. The player has no weapon (only the sentries shoot), for a game pitched as AW plus Crysis. One more problem I reasoned out but did not try: Exo Lab opens full screen, and Forge's key handler (forge.py, FullScreen.escape) takes the first Esc to leave full screen, so Esc may never reach the game's pause menu.
- **The handoff doc is out of date.** The committed WIP.md says 114 tests, with N3, N4, N6 and N7 still open. The commit and the test suite say 181 tests pass and those four are fixed.
