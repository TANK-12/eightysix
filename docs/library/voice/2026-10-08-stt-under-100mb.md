# Speech to text under 100 MB for Forge's Voice app

date: 2026-10-08 · for: both

Relay task 113. Which open-weight speech-to-text model under 100 MB on disk
should be the Voice app's default on this laptop (Intel i5-1245U, 10 cores /
12 threads, Iris Xe shared with Jan, so CPU only)? Every candidate below ran
here on the same 9 clips, CPU, 4 threads.

## The pick

**Whisper base.en q5_1 through whisper.cpp** (`ggml-base.en-q5_1.bin`, 59.7 MB).

- It runs on the engine the Voice app already has (`apps/voice/engines/whisper_cpp.py`),
  so no new engine, no new Python dependency, no torch. Only a new registry row.
- It is the smallest Whisper file here (smaller than the current tiny.en f16
  at 77.7 MB) and has the lower *published* error rate of the two (base vs tiny).
- Measured: 8.1% WER on our 9 clips, real-time factor 0.23 (a 5 s command
  takes about 1.2 s), 0.08 s load, about 200 MB peak RSS.
- On our 9 clips tiny.en scored 7.2% against base q5_1's 8.1%. That gap is
  about 2 words out of about 220, which is noise at this sample size; the
  published LibriSpeech figures (Whisper paper) put base.en ahead of tiny.en.
  If speed matters more than accuracy, tiny.en (RTF 0.13) stays a fine `fast`
  tier and is already in the registry.

The file is already copied to
`~/Forge/models/voice/stt/whisper-base-en-q5/ggml-base.en-q5_1.bin`
(sha256 `4baf70dd0d7c4247ba2b81fafd9c01005ac77c2f9ef064e00dcf195d0e2fdd2f`).

## Figures

Measured here on 2026-10-08 between 21:46 and 21:51, **while other sessions
were running Godot GPU jobs**: `uptime` load averages were 6.7 / 11.3 / 10.5
at the start, 10.2 / 11.2 / 10.6 after the whisper and sherpa runs, and
19.5 / 13.1 / 11.2 after the Whistle run. Speeds are therefore pessimistic and
the Whistle speed in particular was taken under the heaviest load.

WER = word error rate over all 9 clips pooled (jiwer, lowercase, punctuation
stripped). RTF = wall seconds of decoding / seconds of audio, model already
loaded (lower is faster). 97.3 s of audio in total.

| Model | Runtime | Disk MB | WER % | RTF (warm) | Load s | Peak RSS MB | Source of figures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whisper base.en q5_1 (pick) | whisper.cpp CLI | 59.7 | 8.11 | 0.231 | 0.08 | 200 | measured |
| Whisper base.en q8_0 | whisper.cpp CLI | 81.8 | 8.56 | 0.206 | 0.08 | 221 | measured |
| Whisper tiny.en (f16) | whisper.cpp CLI | 77.7 | 7.21 | 0.133 | 0.08 | 178 | measured |
| Cactus Whistle | libneedle3 (ctypes) | 16.9 | 10.81 | 0.509 | 0.03 | 112 | measured (under load 19) |
| Moonshine tiny int8 (sherpa 2026-02-27) | sherpa-onnx 1.13.8 | 44.2 | 69.4 whole clips / 14.9 chunked | 0.017 / 0.020 | 0.53 | 305 | measured |
| Parakeet TDT-CTC 110M int8 | sherpa-onnx 1.13.8 | **131.7** (over limit) | 5.41 | 0.034 | 1.01 | 512 | measured |

Notes on the rows:

- Whisper rows: load and RTF come from whisper-cli's own `whisper_print_timings`
  (load time, and total minus load); each call is a fresh process, which is how
  the Voice app calls it. Load is small because the file sits in the page cache.
  Peak RSS is the largest whisper-cli child.
- Moonshine tiny (sherpa-onnx `from_moonshine_v2`) **returned empty text for 4 of
  the 9 clips** (jfk 11.0 s, ls2 12.5 s, ls3 9.9 s, ls4 29.4 s): onnxruntime
  raised a broadcast error in the decoder's cross-attention for clips around
  10 s and longer. Clips up to 9.2 s decoded. Cutting audio into windows of at
  most 8 s at the quietest point gave 14.9% WER, still the worst of the
  working set, and the cuts add sentence breaks. Very fast, but not safe as a
  default for push-to-talk that can run past 10 s.
- Parakeet 110M int8 is the most accurate and very fast, but its one int8 file
  is 131.7 MB, over the 100 MB limit. If the limit is ever relaxed it is the
  first thing to try (sherpa-onnx `from_nemo_ctc`, no torch, about 0.5 GB RSS).
- Whistle ran within the 10-minute limit by loading its engine library
  directly. It supports 7 languages and 30 s per pass. Its accuracy here sits
  between Whisper and chunked Moonshine; its speed was measured under load 19
  and may be better on a quiet machine. Caveats: the runtime is a closed
  binary (`libneedle3.so` from the `Cactus-Compute/needle3` Hugging Face repo,
  no source), and the official Python client (`cactus-needle` on PyPI) sends
  anonymous telemetry to a Supabase endpoint by default (off with
  `NEEDLE_TELEMETRY=0`). The bench called the C library directly, so nothing
  was sent. Published claims (presenter and cactuscompute.com/blog/whistle):
  about 6x faster decoding than Whisper base and lower error on some
  benchmarks; not reproduced here.

## How it was measured

Clips: `/tmp/voice-stt-clips/` `jfk.wav` (whisper.cpp sample) and `ls0..ls7.wav`
(LibriSpeech test-clean, the sherpa-onnx sample set), 16 kHz mono, 97.3 s in
total; references in `refs.tsv`. Scratch venv `/tmp/voice-stt-bench` (sherpa-onnx
1.13.8, jiwer, soundfile, numpy). whisper-cli is Nyarch's build, the same
binary the Voice app uses. Scripts are in `/tmp/voice-stt-bench2/` (scratch,
may be gone later):

```bash
P=/tmp/voice-stt-bench/bin/python; M=~/Forge/models/voice/stt
uptime
$P bench.py moonshine-tiny ; $P bench.py parakeet
$P bench.py whisper-tiny    $M/whisper-tiny-en/ggml-tiny.en.bin
$P bench.py whisper-base-q8 $M/whisper-base-en-q8/ggml-base.en-q8_0.bin
$P bench.py whisper-base-q5 /tmp/voice-stt-clips/ggml-base.en-q5_1.bin
$P moon_chunk.py                       # Moonshine with <= 8 s windows
NEEDLE_TELEMETRY=0 $P whistle_bench.py # Whistle via libneedle3.so + ctypes
uptime
```

- whisper.cpp: `whisper-cli -m MODEL -f CLIP -t 4 -l en -nt -np` for the text,
  and a second run without `-np` to read the timings.
- sherpa-onnx: `OfflineRecognizer.from_moonshine_v2(...)` /
  `from_nemo_ctc(...)`, `num_threads=4`, one warm-up decode, then all 9 clips
  timed with `time.perf_counter`; peak RSS from `resource.getrusage`.
- Whistle: `needle_load(bytes, len)` then
  `needle_transcribe(samples, n, b"en", None, 0, buf, len(buf))`, JSON result.
- Each candidate ran in its own process, so the RSS figures do not mix.

## Cut audio context for short commands (`short_ctx`), measured 22:12

Whisper's encoder always covers 30 s of audio (1500 frames, 50 a second), so a
1 s command pays for 30 s. whisper-cli's `-ac N` (audio context) cuts that.
Same clips as above plus "What time is it?" (1.1 s, Kokoro voice), base.en
q5_1, 4 threads, load average 9 to 13 from other sessions, so times are rough.
This WER maps "Mr." to "mister", hence 5.3% for the full context where the
table above has 8.1%.

| Audio context | WER, 10 clips | Time, all 10 | "What time is it?" |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| full (1500) | 5.3% (12/226) | 27.7 s | 1.88 s |
| max(256, s*50+128) | 40.7% (92/226): ls1 (4.8 s, N 369) looped, its sentence about eight times | 16.6 s | 0.47 s |
| max(512, s*50+256) | 5.3% (12/226), the same words as full on every clip | 17.0 s | 0.78 s |

Wired (commit 9fde2c1) as the registry row field `"short_ctx": true` on
whisper-base-en-q5, with the second-safe setting max(512, s*50+256) and a loop
guard: a transcript with more than 5 words a second plus 3, or a sentence of 3
or more words twice, is read again with the full context. Real engine at 22:15
under load 13: "What time is it?" 0.89 s cut against 3.17 s full; the 11 s jfk
clip 2.94 against 2.75 s (no gain past about 10 s). Ten clips is a small
sample; the guard is there because a cut context can loop.

## Caveats

- Nine clips (about 220 words) is a small test: WER differences under about
  2 points are within noise. Eight clips are LibriSpeech read speech; real
  push-to-talk (short commands, a laptop mic, a room) will score worse for all.
- Every speed figure was taken under load from other sessions' Godot GPU jobs
  (load average 7 to 19 on 12 threads).
- whisper.cpp reloads the model on every call in the current engine; the
  0.08 s load is from the page cache. A cold first load from disk is slower.
- English only for the pick (the `.en` models). Whistle is the only candidate
  here with other languages.
